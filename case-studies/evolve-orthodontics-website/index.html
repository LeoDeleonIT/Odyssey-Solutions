Website and workflow case study

A website built as part of the practice operation

Odyssey rebuilt the patient-facing experience while coordinating hosting, forms, intake, ownership, and practice-technology touchpoints.

Updated July 29, 2026Client-reported outcomeOrthodontic practice

The practice needed more than a visual redesign. The website had to help prospective patients understand the practice, take the next step, and reach the right internal workflow without creating unclear ownership around hosting, forms, or follow-up.

EngagementWebsite and workflow rebuild
Primary usersPatients and practice staff
Primary needClear action and ownership

The business problem

A healthcare website sits between marketing and operations. A patient may begin with a service page, move to a form or scheduling option, and expect a timely response. The practice needs to know what happens at every step, which system receives the request, and which team member owns follow-up.

The patient path needed clarity

Visitors needed a simpler route from learning about treatment to taking the right next action.

Forms needed a defined purpose

Every form field and destination had to support a real workflow instead of collecting information without a clear owner.

Hosting affected operations

Performance, updates, security, and maintenance needed assigned responsibility after launch.

Follow-up crossed systems

The website had to fit the practice’s approved intake and technology processes rather than operate in isolation.

How Odyssey approached the work

  1. Map the patient journeyOrganize content and calls to action around what prospective patients need to understand and do next.
  2. Design the intake pathConnect forms and inquiry routes to the practice’s approved follow-up workflow with clear internal ownership.
  3. Rebuild the experienceCreate a responsive site designed for clarity, accessibility, performance, and use on mobile devices.
  4. Coordinate hosting and systemsTreat hosting, maintenance, forms, and practice-technology touchpoints as parts of the same delivery.
  5. Prepare for ongoing ownershipKeep updates, security decisions, and workflow changes from becoming orphaned after launch.

What the practice received

Rebuilt patient-facing website
Clearer service and action paths
Responsive mobile experience
Coordinated hosting ownership
Defined intake workflow touchpoints
Ongoing performance and maintenance focus
Client-reported outcome The practice owner reported a noticeable increase in new-patient inquiries.

The owner also valued Odyssey’s approach to the website as operational infrastructure rather than an isolated marketing project.

Why the change mattered

A stronger website does not stop at launch. It gives patients a clearer experience and gives the practice a maintainable path for receiving, routing, and responding to interest. That requires content, design, hosting, security, and internal follow-up to have owners.

Privacy boundary: Website forms and third-party services must be evaluated before collecting sensitive information. A vendor or hosting label alone does not make every workflow HIPAA compliant. This client-reported outcome is not a guarantee of inquiry volume or business performance.

What another practice can learn

Before rebuilding a healthcare website, define the full path after a visitor selects a call to action. Decide what information is necessary, where it goes, who responds, which systems are approved, and who maintains the experience. A polished page without that operating path is only half a solution.
