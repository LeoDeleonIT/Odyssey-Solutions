Office Phones Down at a Dental or Medical Practice? A Same-Day Checklist

When the office phones stop working, check scope, power and internet, call the provider and forward calls to a mobile phone. A same-day checklist for practices.

Published November 18, 20263-minute readPublished by Odyssey Solutions

For a dental or medical practice, the phone is the front door. When it stops working, patients cannot confirm appointments, call about pain or reach the office in an emergency, and the front desk is left guessing.

Most office phones today run over the internet, so phone outages often start with the network. Work through this checklist to find the cause quickly and keep calls coming in while it is fixed.

Check scope in two minutes

Find out what is actually down. Are all phones affected or only one? Can staff make outgoing calls, receive incoming calls, or neither? Is the internet working on the office computers? Call the main number from a mobile phone and note exactly what a caller hears: ringing, a busy signal, voicemail or an error message.

Those answers separate a single-phone problem, an office network problem and a provider problem. Write them down; the provider and IT will ask for them.

Check power and the network

Many desk phones get their power through the network cable from a switch. If every phone is dark, check that the network switch and router have power and their lights are on. A tripped power strip or a switch on a failed battery backup can take every phone down at once.

If the internet is down for the computers too, the phones are almost certainly down because of it. Restart the internet modem and router once, then contact the internet provider. A practice with backup internet should confirm that the failover connection actually took over; our backup internet failover test explains how to check.

Keep calls coming in

If the problem is with the phone service rather than the office, call the phone provider’s support line and check its status page. Ask them to forward the main number to a mobile phone while the outage lasts. Many hosted phone services also let an administrator set this forwarding from a web portal, so it helps to know who has that login before you need it.

Update the practice’s website, online booking page or social media with a short note and the temporary number if the outage will last more than an hour. Make sure whoever answers the forwarded calls has the schedule available.

Remember 911 during an outage

Office phone systems with multiple lines are covered by federal 911 rules: Kari’s Law requires that users can dial 911 directly without a prefix, and the RAY BAUM’S Act rules require the system to send a dispatchable location, such as the street address and suite number, with a 911 call. FCC: 911 requirements for multi-line telephone systems

When the office phones are down, those protections are down too. Tell staff to use a mobile phone for any emergency and to give the full address and suite number to the dispatcher. The FCC explains how 911 and enhanced 911 location work on its 911 services page. FCC: 911 and E911 services

Fix the cause afterward

Once service is back, record what failed and how long it lasted. Common follow-ups are battery backup for the router, switch and phone equipment, a second internet connection with tested failover, and a written forwarding procedure with the provider’s support number and portal access stored where the office manager can find them.
