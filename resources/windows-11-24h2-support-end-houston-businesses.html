Windows 11 24H2 Support Ends Soon for Home and Pro

Published October 2, 20264-minute readPublished by Odyssey Solutions · Guidance for Greater Houston businesses.

If your business computers still run Windows 11 version 24H2 Home or Pro, schedule their next supported feature update now. Those editions reach the end of updates in mid-October 2026. Check Microsoft's release-health notice and edition-specific lifecycle listing for the current cutoff before scheduling. After the applicable deadline, affected devices will no longer receive the updates and support described in the notice, including monthly security updates.

The first job is to find the affected computers. A spreadsheet that says only “Windows 11” cannot answer that question. Your team needs the edition and version, followed by an update plan that accounts for the software people use to do their jobs.

Check both the edition and version

On an individual computer, open Settings, choose System, then About. Under Windows specifications, record the edition and version. Microsoft documents these steps in its device information guide.

For a larger fleet, ask IT for an inventory report. Include shared reception computers, conference-room PCs, spare laptops, remote employees, and machines that have not checked in recently. An offline device belongs on the follow-up list until someone confirms its status.

Microsoft's release schedule gives Enterprise and Education editions different support periods. Do not apply the Home and Pro deadline to every Windows 11 device. Have IT check the exact edition, version, and servicing channel against the appropriate schedule.

Choose the target with your applications in mind

Ask your IT provider to name the supported Windows release planned for each affected device. Then identify applications that need a compatibility check: accounting software, estimating tools, document-management systems, VPN clients, printers, scanners, and anything connected to specialized equipment.

For each important application, capture the vendor's supported Windows versions and any prerequisite updates. If the vendor's answer is unclear, make that an assigned question with a due date. Avoid leaving the office manager to coordinate conflicting advice from several vendors during an interrupted workday.

The release number on a newer computer is not enough to choose a deployment target. For example, Microsoft's release information says version 26H1 is designed for certain new devices and is not offered as an in-place feature update from 24H2 or 25H2. IT should choose an eligible path for the computers you actually own.

Test a representative computer first

Select a pilot device that reflects normal work. Ask a staff member to test a short list of real tasks after the update: sign in, open the main application, retrieve a shared document, print, scan, connect remotely, and join a call.

Record whether each task passed and who checked it. If different teams use different software or hardware, one successful laptop may not represent the whole office. Add a pilot for each materially different setup.

Before installation, confirm that important business data is covered by the recovery plan and that an authorized administrator can access any required recovery information. Agree on what happens if a computer cannot return to service on time. A prepared spare or temporary workstation may be part of that plan.

Investigate a blocked update

If a device is not being offered the intended release, ask IT to identify the reason. It could involve an update policy, device readiness, or a known compatibility issue.

Microsoft uses safeguard holds to prevent affected devices from receiving feature updates with known issues. Microsoft recommends waiting for the issue to be resolved and the hold released rather than attempting a manual update. A missing update should trigger investigation, not an improvised workaround by the employee using the computer.

If a device cannot be updated before support ends, document the blocker, business impact, responsible vendor, and next decision. Have IT explain the options and interim risk controls to the business owner.

Close the work with evidence

Schedule the rollout around the people and applications affected. Tell staff when to save their work, whether a restart is expected, and whom to contact if something fails.

Afterward, request a simple completion report:

  • Devices moved to the approved supported release
  • Devices still pending, with an owner and next action
  • Important workflow tests and unresolved problems
  • Devices that were missing from the original inventory

Keep these checks in your ongoing business computer maintenance plan, so the next support deadline appears in a regular review.
