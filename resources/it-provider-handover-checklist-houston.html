How to Prepare an IT Provider Handover

Published October 5, 20264-minute readPublished by Odyssey Solutions · Guidance for Greater Houston businesses.

Before your current IT provider finishes its work, make sure the incoming team can support the business using verified access and current documentation. A handover should establish who can administer each important system, who receives its alerts, and who will respond when something fails.

Start with an agreed transition plan. The outgoing provider, incoming team, and a business decision-maker should know the effective date, the responsibilities during any overlap, and the conditions for completing the change. The UK National Cyber Security Centre's small-business MSP guidance recommends clear responsibilities and attention to contract duration and exit arrangements. Those are useful planning principles; your own agreement determines the applicable terms. NCSC provider guidance

Assign a business owner to the transition

Choose someone who can approve the schedule, resolve missing information, and decide which unresolved issues can be accepted. That person does not need to perform the technical work. They need a clear view of what remains open.

Create a short handover register. Each entry should identify the system, its purpose, the current administrator, the incoming owner, the evidence received, and the next action. Start with services that would stop trading or serving customers if access failed.

For example, a two-office business may need separate entries for its email tenant, each site's network, shared files, line-of-business application, telephone service, and backups. A single entry labeled "IT passwords" would hide too many different dependencies.

Verify administrative access before the final day

Have authorized administrators demonstrate that the business can reach the systems it owns. Check both the sign-in and the permissions needed for an agreed task. Confirm that account recovery does not depend entirely on a departing technician's personal device or inbox.

Microsoft recommends at least two emergency access accounts for Microsoft Entra organizations, protected with strong authentication, securely controlled, monitored, and regularly tested. These privileged accounts require deliberate administration. They should not become ordinary shared logins for staff. Microsoft emergency access guidance

For the handover, ask who is authorized to use the emergency process, where its instructions are securely held, and when it was last validated. Have the incoming administrator check that alerts reach the correct team. Keep passwords, recovery material, and secret keys out of the general handover spreadsheet and ordinary email.

Separate Microsoft 365 administration from purchasing

Changing who supports Microsoft 365 may involve more than one relationship. Microsoft's Partner relationships page distinguishes administrative permissions from the reseller relationship. Removing a partner's admin roles does not itself remove that commercial relationship. Microsoft partner relationship guidance

Ask the incoming team to document the current partners, delegated roles, relevant expiry dates, and purchasing arrangements. Confirm who will supply licenses, handle renewal questions, and receive billing notices. Review the permissions requested by the replacement provider before approving them.

Treat license continuity and access removal as separate work items with coordinated dates. Do not cancel a subscription simply to remove an old provider's administrative access. If an agreement or purchasing arrangement is unclear, resolve it with the appropriate supplier before making a change that could interrupt users.

Request records that help the next technician work

Ask for a usable operational record, then have the incoming team validate it. A practical package may include:

  • Device inventory with locations and support status
  • Network diagram, circuit references, and configuration backup locations
  • Application inventory with vendor support contacts
  • Backup scope, storage destinations, and recent restore-test results
  • Monitoring and security tools, their owners, and alert destinations
  • Open incidents, recurring problems, and upcoming maintenance

The record should distinguish what is known from what still needs checking. Have the incoming technician trace one important workflow through the documentation. Can they identify the workstation, application, network dependency, vendor, and recovery arrangement without relying on the outgoing technician's memory?

Record gaps explicitly. A missing configuration export or an unexplained recurring alert deserves an owner and due date.

Keep protection and recovery working during the change

Agree on the order for replacing management, security, monitoring, and backup tools. Confirm when the new team becomes responsible for responding to alerts and how it will verify coverage. Avoid leaving a period in which both providers assume the other is watching.

Ask about historical backups as well as new ones. Establish who controls access, which copies remain available, what software is required to restore them, and whether any service ends with the old agreement. Have the receiving team perform an approved representative recovery test before relying on the new arrangement.

Use a written test result rather than a dashboard screenshot alone. It should identify what was recovered, whether it was usable, and any limitation that affects the business's recovery plan. CISA also recommends regularly testing backup availability and integrity in a recovery scenario. CISA recovery guidance

Finish with a documented acceptance check

Before closing the transition, verify that staff know the new support contact and that a test support request reaches the correct team. Confirm approved administrative access, critical workflow tests, alert routing, recovery evidence, and the remaining issues.

Then have authorized administrators remove or revise the departing provider's accounts, delegated permissions, remote tools, and other access according to the agreed plan. Review service-account dependencies before rotating credentials. Record the changes and confirm the business continues to operate afterward.
