A named consultant and documented ownership

Know who you are working with—and what Odyssey owns

Odyssey Solutions helps Houston dental practices, healthcare organizations, and growing businesses build reliable, secure technology around the work their people perform.

Technology becomes difficult when ownership is fragmented. The network vendor points to the software vendor, the software vendor points to the workstation, and the person responsible for the business is left coordinating the response. Odyssey was built to close those gaps with a named point of contact and documented responsibilities.

Accountability is defined before delivery. The proposed scope identifies what Odyssey will own, what the client must decide or provide, what remains with third-party vendors, and how issues should be escalated. That ownership model—not an unsupported promise—is the foundation for the work.

From consultation to delivery

1. Understand the operation

Odyssey starts with the people, workflows, systems, recurring problems, upcoming changes, and consequences of downtime. If Odyssey is not the right fit for the requested work, the boundary should be clear before a project or service agreement begins.

2. Map ownership and dependencies

Odyssey documents the relevant users, devices, cloud services, networks, software vendors, security controls, backups, contracts, and escalation paths instead of treating each symptom as an isolated ticket.

3. Agree on a practical scope

The organization receives a defined next step tied to operating impact, risk, maintainability, available resources, and the responsibilities Odyssey can reasonably accept.

4. Keep delivery accountable

During delivery, Odyssey coordinates the work inside its scope, tracks external vendor dependencies, documents material decisions, and makes unresolved ownership visible to the people responsible for the organization.
