Build a Repeatable IT Setup as Your Team Grows

A practical startup checklist for the first 10 to 50 employees: accounts, devices, Microsoft 365, access, vendors, backups, and the support model that fits the work.

Published September 23, 20268-minute readBusiness IT guidePublished by Odyssey Solutions · Guidance for Greater Houston businesses.

The first few employee devices and accounts are often set up informally. That can work for a small team, but repeated growth makes a simple, documented process valuable. The goal is not to buy every tool at once. It is to make ownership, access, onboarding, recovery, and support repeatable before the next hire or change creates confusion.

Build a foundation before adding tools: name who owns the company accounts, standardize the employee setup process, and document how the team gets help when a device or account does not work.

1. Establish account and email ownership

Document who owns the company's Microsoft 365 tenant, domain registration, billing accounts, administrator accounts, shared mailboxes, cloud storage, and vendor contracts. Use more than one appropriate business owner or recovery contact where the platform allows it, and maintain the record in a place the business can access if one person is unavailable.

The Microsoft 365 support guide for Houston small businesses covers tenant ownership, identity, collaboration, devices, and recovery questions in more detail.

2. Standardize employee devices and inventory

Choose a small set of approved device types, record serial numbers and assigned users, and define what is included when someone starts. At a minimum, record the computer, charger, phone if company-managed, key accessories, operating system, warranty information, and work location. A basic inventory makes replacement, support, and offboarding easier.

Use the business computer maintenance checklist to plan routine updates and upkeep after setup.

3. Make onboarding and offboarding repeatable

Create a checklist for every new employee: manager approval, work account, MFA setup, device issue, shared-resource access, acceptable-use guidance, vendor access if needed, and a support contact. Create a separate departure checklist for access removal, data handoff, device return, shared mailbox decisions, and recovery of business-owned accounts.

Microsoft documents steps for securing former employee access and retaining necessary business data. Odyssey's small-business onboarding and offboarding checklist provides a planning framework for the complete process.

4. Set a practical access baseline

Require individual accounts and MFA where possible. CISA recommends MFA for email, file storage, remote access, and administrative accounts, starting with the systems that would create the greatest disruption if access were lost. Decide who can grant access, reset credentials, approve administrator rights, and contact each vendor.

  • Use individual identities instead of shared credentials.
  • Record administrator and recovery contacts for each important service.
  • Test a normal sign-in and a recovery process before an urgent situation.
  • Review access when a person's job changes or ends.

5. Document backup, vendors, and recovery

List the systems that hold work the team would need after a device loss, account issue, accidental deletion, or office disruption. Document which vendor owns each system, who receives alerts, who can approve a restore, and how the business will test recovery. CISA's small-business resources include backup, logging, password, and MFA guidance.

The free Odyssey IT Resilience Toolkit includes practical templates for organizing policies, downtime steps, and recovery responsibilities.

6. Choose between a project and ongoing support

A setup project can create the initial accounts, device process, Microsoft 365 baseline, network plan, and documentation. Ongoing support can maintain the process as people join, systems change, vendors need coordination, and regular maintenance continues. Some businesses keep a technical owner internally and bring in support for defined projects or shared responsibilities.

Use the outsourced versus in-house IT comparison to identify what stays with the business and what an outside provider should own.

Questions to answer before the next hire

  1. Who owns the domain, Microsoft 365 tenant, billing accounts, and administrator access?
  2. What device and account setup does every employee receive?
  3. How does a manager request access, and who approves it?
  4. What happens to access, devices, and business data when a person leaves?
  5. Which systems need recovery planning, and when was a practical restore last tested?
  6. Which work is a one-time setup project, and which requires ongoing ownership?
