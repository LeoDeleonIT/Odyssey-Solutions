Suspicious Google Calendar Invitation? What Your Business Should Do

Handle suspicious Google Calendar invitations safely, report eligible spam, review invitation settings and check that legitimate business meetings still arrive.

Published October 14, 20265-minute readPublished by Odyssey Solutions

An unfamiliar event on your work calendar deserves the same care as an unexpected email. A meeting title, reminder or familiar-looking logo does not establish that the organizer is legitimate. Before opening the event's links, downloading an attachment or calling a number in its description, check what arrived and report it through your business's normal support process.

An unwanted invitation alone does not establish that someone signed into your account. Google Calendar has settings that control which invitations are automatically added. The first task is to understand the event and its source, then choose the appropriate response.

Verify the request without using its contact details

Look at the organizer's address and ask whether the meeting matches real work. A vague security warning, unexpected invoice discussion or urgent request for account access should be checked separately. If the event appears to come from a known vendor, use a previously verified contact or the vendor's known website to confirm it.

That independent route follows the FTC's phishing guidance: verify suspicious requests using contact information you already know is genuine, rather than the information supplied in the message. Applying that principle to a calendar invitation avoids treating the event description as a trusted source. FTC guidance on recognizing phishing

Do not forward a suspicious invitation to the whole team as a warning. Send the relevant details through the organization's approved reporting method. Include the organizer, event title, approximate arrival time and whether anyone interacted with it. Keep other appointments and private meeting details out of screenshots.

Report eligible spam in Google Calendar

On a computer, open Google Calendar through your usual trusted route. Google's instructions say to select the suspicious event, open More actions and choose Report as spam. The option applies to events sent from Google Calendar; events created by another provider, app or service cannot be reported through that same feature.

Reporting removes the event from your calendar. If it is recurring, the events in that series are removed. Preserve any information your IT team needs before taking that step. If the reporting option is unavailable, ask the administrator to identify the source and correct handling method rather than accepting the invitation to investigate it. Google's calendar-spam reporting instructions

A missing button does not mean the event is safe. It may simply be a different type of event or a different provider.

Choose invitation settings that fit the job

In Google Calendar on a computer, Settings, Event settings, Add invitations to my calendar provides several choices. “Only if the sender is known” automatically adds events from contacts, people in your organization and people you have previously interacted with. “When I respond to the invitation in email” waits for that response. Your administrator may restrict the available choices.

Google says these settings apply to new invitations. Unknown invitations that are not automatically added can still arrive by email. Selecting known senders can also reveal to a sender that they are not in your contacts. Google's invitation-setting guidance

Consider the employee's workflow before choosing a setting. A receptionist who receives first-time customer appointments needs a reliable way to review unfamiliar invitations. A project coordinator may receive meetings from several external vendors. Decide who will review the invitation inbox, how often, and what to do when an expected meeting does not appear.

“Known” is a display rule, not a guarantee that every future request from that account is appropriate. Continue checking unusual requests, even when their events appear automatically.

Understand what blocking changes

For repeated unwanted invitations, Google also provides organizer blocking on a computer. Its guidance distinguishes Google Accounts from non-Google addresses and explains that blocking can affect other Google services, including Chat, Drive and Photos.

Blocking does not revoke calendar access that was previously shared with that person. It also does not make all existing invitations disappear as a group. These are separate cleanup and access questions, so a business should review the effect before blocking a customer, supplier or former collaborator. Google's organizer-blocking guidance

If the employee is acting as someone else's delegate or the event belongs to a shared calendar, involve the calendar owner or administrator. Avoid deleting a shared business calendar simply to remove unwanted content.

Escalate if someone already interacted

Tell IT promptly if anyone followed a link, entered credentials, approved a sign-in request, installed software or supplied business information. Describe the actual action and time without including the password or verification code in the report.

Those details matter more than a broad statement that “the calendar was hacked.” The security team can assess account activity and decide what containment or recovery is necessary. Removing an event is not evidence that an exposed account or device has been secured.

If unwanted events keep returning, have the administrator examine how they are being created. On Android, Google's guidance includes checking which apps have calendar access; removing permission does not itself delete existing events. The appropriate response depends on the source, not just the event's appearance. Google's Android calendar-spam guidance

Check that legitimate scheduling still works

After an approved settings change, test an ordinary invitation with a colleague and, where appropriate, an authorized external contact. Verify where the invitation appears, who reviews it and how it becomes an accepted meeting. Use a clearly labeled harmless test and remove it afterward through the normal process.
