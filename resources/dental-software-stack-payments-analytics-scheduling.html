Dental Software Stack: Payments, Analytics, Scheduling & Claims

Evaluate each business tool by workflow, supported integration, data ownership, security, and support responsibility. A feature list alone is not enough.

Published July 20, 20269-minute readTechnology-selection guidePublished by Odyssey Solutions · Dental technology integration and support guidance.

A dental practice may use separate systems for practice management, imaging, scheduling, payments, claims, referral management, reviews, analytics, communications, and documents. Each product can solve a real problem, but the complete stack can create duplicated data, unclear ownership, excessive access, and support gaps.

Selection rule: start with the operating workflow and source of truth. Then confirm the exact integration, version, data direction, failure handling, security, and support responsibility in writing.

Map the workflow before choosing tools

Follow a patient or business event from beginning to end: discovery and booking, registration, clinical documentation, imaging, treatment acceptance, claims, payment, referral, recall, review, and reporting. Identify where data begins, where it changes, which system is authoritative, and where staff re-enter information.

Understand common product roles

  • MyDentalForce: a multi-location operations platform currently advertising direct Open Dental API synchronization for analytics, referrals, new patients, claims oversight, goals, reviews, documents, and reporting.
  • Practice by Numbers: a dental business platform advertising analytics and additional operational, marketing, payment, communications, and patient-relationship capabilities across supported practice-management systems.
  • Zocdoc: provider profiles, patient discovery, online booking, and supported scheduling connections.
  • Crown by Moolah: a dental-focused payment offering whose exact device, software, and integration requirements should be confirmed for the practice environment.
  • EDS: a separate claims-clearinghouse provider offering claims, eligibility, status, remittance, payment, statement, and attachment services. Treat it as a normal third-party vendor with its own contract and support path.
  • Henry Schein products: a broad vendor portfolio. Record the exact product, version, hosting model, integration, and authorized support contact rather than treating the brand as one system.

Verify integrations instead of assuming them

“Integrates with” can mean a real-time API, scheduled sync, file export, browser link, one-way bridge, or manual process. Ask what data moves, in which direction, how often, under whose credentials, and what happens when the connection fails. Confirm whether the integration is supported by both vendors for the exact versions in use.

Control identity, data, and vendor access

Use unique accounts, least-privilege roles, MFA where available, documented onboarding and termination, and protected administrator access. Inventory which vendors create, receive, maintain, or transmit ePHI and evaluate agreements and Business Associate responsibilities with qualified privacy or legal guidance.

Remote support should use approved tools, named technicians or attributable accounts, encryption, logging, and timely removal. Avoid shared credentials passed among staff and vendors.

Define ownership and exit requirements

The practice should know who owns accounts, domains, phone numbers, merchant relationships, configurations, data exports, and integration credentials. Contracts should describe usable data export, retention, termination, transition assistance, and what happens to connected systems if one subscription ends.

Monitor business outcomes and technical health

Analytics accuracy depends on stable integrations and consistent definitions. Reconcile key totals against the source system, monitor failed syncs, review inactive accounts, and assign owners for exceptions. Remote monitoring can watch servers, workstations, backups, and networks, but application-specific integration health may require vendor dashboards and operational review.

Score each product against the same criteria

  • The tool solves a documented workflow problem.
  • The exact integration and supported versions are confirmed.
  • Data ownership, export, and retention are clear.
  • Access, MFA, logs, vendor access, and BAA needs are evaluated.
  • Implementation, training, support, and escalation owners are named.
  • Failure and downtime procedures are tested.
  • Total cost includes devices, interfaces, migration, support, and staff time.
