Microsoft 365 Sharing Link Cleanup After a Project Ends

Published October 2, 20264-minute readPublished by Odyssey Solutions · Guidance for Greater Houston businesses.

The project is finished, the final invoice is filed, and the outside team has moved on. The shared folders deserve a closeout too.

For a Greater Houston small business, a useful review starts with one completed project. Decide who still needs its files, identify how they get access, and remove access the business no longer approves. An office manager can organize the decisions while the file owner or IT administrator handles permission changes.

1 Name the owner and define what stays available

Choose a business owner who can approve access decisions. Record the project, its main folders, the people who still need the records, and the person responsible for future requests.

Ask whether anyone still needs to finish billing, answer customer questions, or deliver a final revision. Give temporary exceptions a named approver and a review date.

Keep access cleanup separate from decisions to delete records. If important files live in an employee's OneDrive, ask IT to review their long-term home and ownership before moving anything.

2 Find the files behind the links

Collect the project links from handoff emails, meeting notes, and Teams conversations. Open the actual OneDrive or SharePoint location and review the parent folder as well as individually shared documents.

For each location, keep a short working list:

  • File or folder and business owner
  • Link audience and view or edit permission
  • People or groups with another route to access
  • Proposed change, approval, and test result

Treat this as an inventory for the project. A few familiar links are not evidence that every related file has been reviewed.

3 Read the audience on each link

Microsoft distinguishes several link options:

  • Anyone links can work for people outside the company who receive the link
  • People in your organization links can work for colleagues who obtain the link, beyond the original recipients
  • Specific people links limit that link to named recipients, while other permissions may still exist
  • People with existing access links do not grant new permission

Start with broad links used for temporary collaboration. Ask whether each audience still matches a business need. A link that was convenient during a project may now reach more people than the owner intends. See Microsoft’s sharing-options guidance.

4 Check permissions beyond the link

Select a file or folder and open Manage access, available through its Details pane. Review the links and the people with access. Microsoft documents sharing links, direct access, and access through the site as separate ways to reach content. Removing one link therefore may leave another permission in place. Microsoft’s permission-management instructions explain these controls.

If the controls are unavailable in your account, ask the site owner or IT administrator to review them.

Ask IT to trace group or inherited access before changing it. Microsoft 365 group membership can provide access to the SharePoint site and other connected services. Removing someone from that group can affect work beyond this project. See Microsoft’s group and site permissions guidance.

If the permission belongs to a larger department folder, stop and get the relevant owner's approval. Record the unresolved access path rather than making a broad change to complete the checklist.

5 Make approved changes in a controlled order

Agree on a change window with the people who still use the files. Have the authorized owner or administrator remove obsolete links and direct permissions from the approved list.

When access must continue, use the approved recipients and permission level. If an existing link cannot be changed from edit to view, replace it with an appropriately configured link and retire the old one. Microsoft describes link removal and direct-access changes in its permission-management instructions.

Tell continuing users which link to use. Keep a change log identifying the item, approver, change, and date. Avoid a blanket stop-sharing action when some collaboration still needs to continue.

6 Verify the handoff

Have a continuing user confirm that required files open and that their permission level supports their work. Ask IT to verify removed access through the relevant access paths; testing only with the owner's account proves little about another person's permissions.

Recheck any exceptions on their agreed dates. Add this review to the project closeout checklist, with one person accountable for completing it.
