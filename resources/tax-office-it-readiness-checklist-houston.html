Prepare Your Accounting or Tax Office Technology Before Peak Season

A practical readiness checklist for Houston accounting and tax offices preparing staff, devices, accounts, vendors, and support before their busiest work begins.

Published September 23, 20267-minute readBusiness IT guidePublished by Odyssey Solutions · Guidance for Greater Houston businesses.

Peak filing work puts more pressure on the everyday technology an accounting or tax office already uses. New and returning staff may need devices and individual accounts. Printers, scanners, shared files, internet service, vendor contacts, and backup responsibilities need to be ready before the first urgent request arrives.

Start two to four weeks early: name one person to own the checklist, list the people and systems needed for peak work, and test the routine steps before the office is busy.

1. Confirm people, roles, and start dates

List permanent staff, seasonal workers, contractors, and managers who will need access. Record the start and end date, supervisor, work location, device, and the business systems each role needs. Do not share a generic account to save setup time. Individual accounts make access changes and troubleshooting easier to manage.

Use the existing small-business IT onboarding and offboarding checklist to coordinate account setup, equipment, and departure steps.

2. Prepare workstations and office equipment

Confirm that each person has a working device, charger, network connection, required updates, and a practical place to work. Test printers, scanners, shared folders, internet connectivity, and any office equipment that staff must use in a typical client workflow. Keep the manufacturer or provider support contact with the equipment record.

A maintenance plan should cover routine updates and basic equipment checks throughout the year. The business computer maintenance checklist can help assign those recurring tasks.

3. Set up accounts and secure access

Create individual work accounts before the employee starts and give each person only the access needed for the role. Confirm ownership of the Microsoft 365 tenant, administrator accounts, shared mailboxes, file storage, and recovery contacts. CISA recommends requiring multifactor authentication where possible, beginning with email, file storage, remote access, and administrative accounts.

  • Confirm who can add users, reset access, approve purchases, and contact vendors.
  • Turn on MFA using the strongest available method that fits the system and team.
  • Test a normal sign-in and an account-recovery path before peak work.
  • Record where shared documents live and who owns them.

For a fuller account and collaboration review, see Microsoft 365 support for Houston small businesses.

4. Verify backup and recovery ownership

Know which business records are covered by each backup or recovery arrangement, who receives alerts, who can approve a restore, and how staff would continue priority work if a device, account, or office connection fails. A backup is not a recovery plan until the office has tested a realistic request and documented the result.

Use the Microsoft 365 backup guide and the business backup and disaster recovery guide to organize those questions.

5. Keep a vendor and escalation record

Maintain one current record of the internet carrier, phone provider, copier or scanner provider, software vendors, building contacts, and IT support contacts. For each one, note the account owner, support method, approved contact, equipment or service affected, and escalation path. CISA's small-business vendor guidance includes questions businesses can use when a provider needs logical or physical access to systems.

6. Choose the right help model

A defined project can prepare devices, accounts, documentation, or an office network before the season. Ongoing IT support can help with staff changes, daily troubleshooting, Microsoft 365, devices, vendors, backups, and regular maintenance across the year. The right choice depends on who owns day-to-day work after the readiness project ends.

Compare the ownership questions in outsourced versus in-house IT for Houston businesses before deciding how responsibilities will be shared.

Questions to answer before peak season

  1. Which people start, change roles, or leave during the busy period?
  2. Which accounts, devices, printers, scanners, files, and connections must work on day one?
  3. Who owns each administrator account and vendor relationship?
  4. How will staff get help if an account, device, or office connection fails?
  5. What backup or recovery step has the office actually tested?
