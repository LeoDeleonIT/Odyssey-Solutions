How to Get Rid of Scam Alerts on Windows

Fake "your PC is infected" pop-ups usually come from a site allowed to send notifications. Remove it in Chrome or Edge, then run a quick scan.

Published November 3, 20263-minute readPublished by Odyssey Solutions

A pop-up in the corner of the screen says your PC is infected and tells you to call Microsoft right now. Don't click it, and don't call the number. Microsoft doesn't put phone numbers in error messages or warnings, and it won't call you out of the blue to fix your computer. Microsoft on tech support scams

The short video above walks through every step in about a minute. The written version is below if you'd rather follow along.

Where these alerts come from

Most of the time the computer isn't infected. At some point a website asked to show notifications, and someone clicked Allow. That site can now send pop-ups to the corner of the screen whenever the browser is running, even when the site itself isn't open. Scam sites use that permission to push fake virus warnings, fake "subscription expired" notices and phone numbers.

You can usually tell because the alert says it came from Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge, with a website name underneath it. Real Windows Security alerts come from Windows Security, not from a website.

Turn them off in Chrome

  1. Click the three dots in the top-right corner of Chrome and choose Settings.
  2. Go to Privacy and security, then Site settings.
  3. Under Permissions, click Notifications.
  4. Look under Allowed to send notifications.
  5. For any site you don't recognize, click the three dots next to it and choose Remove.

If you never want sites asking, you can also choose Don't allow sites to send notifications on the same page. Keep sites you actually use, like your email or calendar, if you rely on their alerts. Google's notification settings help

Turn them off in Microsoft Edge

It's the same idea in Edge. Open Settings, go to Cookies and site permissions, then Notifications. Under Allow, remove any site you don't recognize. Microsoft's Edge notification help

If more than one browser is installed, check each one. A site allowed in Chrome can keep sending alerts even after you clean up Edge.

Run a quick scan to be safe

Once the alerts are gone, check that nothing else came along with them.

  1. Click Start and type Windows Security, then open it.
  2. Go to Virus & threat protection.
  3. Click Quick scan and let it finish.

If the scan finds something, follow the steps Windows Security gives you, or have your IT team look at it before you keep working. Microsoft on Windows Security

If you already called the number

This is when it matters to move fast. If you called, or let someone connect to your computer:

  • Unplug the network cable or turn off Wi-Fi so they can't stay connected.
  • Call your IT team right away and tell them what you saw, what you clicked and whether anyone connected remotely.
  • From a different device, change the passwords for anything you signed in to while they were connected, starting with email and banking.
  • If you paid them anything, call your bank or card company and tell them it was fraud.
  • You can report the scam to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

For an office that handles patient or client information, a remote session from a scammer is a security incident, not just an annoying pop-up. Treat it that way and let IT check the machine before it goes back on the network.

Stop it from happening again

  • Click Block when a website you don't know asks to show notifications.
  • Keep Chrome and Edge updated and restart them when they ask.
  • Remind the front desk that real support never starts with a pop-up and a phone number.
