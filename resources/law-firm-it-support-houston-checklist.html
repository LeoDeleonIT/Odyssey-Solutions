How Houston Law Firms Can Evaluate IT Support

Use this practical checklist to organize staff support, access, devices, Microsoft 365, vendors, backups, documentation, and escalation before choosing an IT provider.

Published September 23, 20268-minute readBusiness IT guidePublished by Odyssey Solutions · Guidance for Greater Houston businesses.

A law firm does not need to know every technical detail before evaluating IT support. It does need a clear picture of the people, devices, accounts, vendors, office systems, and work patterns the firm depends on. That picture helps a provider understand whether the firm needs ongoing support, a defined project, or an initial technology review.

Begin with ownership: write down who owns accounts, approves changes, manages vendors, receives alerts, and decides when outside support should be involved.

1. Decide whether the need is ongoing support or a defined project

Ongoing support can cover recurring staff requests, account changes, Microsoft 365 administration, device upkeep, network work, backups, vendor coordination, and documentation. A defined project may focus on an office move, device replacement, network change, account cleanup, or a transition to a new support provider. A firm can begin with a project and then decide whether ongoing support is useful.

For a broader comparison of support models, read outsourced versus in-house IT for Houston businesses.

2. Build a simple technology inventory

List the office locations, staff roles, workstations, laptops, shared mailboxes, file storage, internet service, printers, scanners, phones, network equipment, and important business vendors. The goal is not a perfect technical diagram. The goal is to identify what must be understood before a change, outage, staff transition, or vendor escalation.

  • Identify the person authorized to approve account, device, or vendor changes.
  • Keep administrator account ownership documented and recoverable.
  • Record vendors, account numbers, service contacts, and support methods in an internal record.
  • Include remote workers and any devices that access firm resources away from the office.

3. Ask how access and staff changes are handled

Ask the provider how new employees receive accounts, devices, shared resources, and the access needed for their role. Ask how the process changes when a person moves roles or leaves. Microsoft documents a sequence for securing former employee access and retaining necessary business data, while CISA recommends MFA for business email, file storage, remote access, and administrative accounts where possible.

4. Clarify backup and recovery responsibilities

Ask which systems and data are covered by each backup or recovery arrangement, who checks alerts, who authorizes a restore, and how the firm would test a priority workflow after an outage. A provider should be able to explain its role without making assumptions about applications, vendors, or business records it does not manage.

Use the business backup and disaster recovery guide to prepare a practical recovery discussion.

5. Define vendor coordination and escalation

Different vendors may own different pieces of the environment. A software provider may own its application. An internet carrier may own connectivity. An IT provider may own the supporting devices, network, identity, or troubleshooting process. Ask who opens the ticket, who provides technical details, who communicates with staff, and when responsibility is handed off.

CISA's vendor guidance gives small and midsize businesses a useful starting point for assessing providers who need access to systems or facilities.

Questions to ask an IT provider

  1. Which recurring tasks are included, and which work is treated as a separate project?
  2. Who will own user accounts, administrative access, devices, documentation, and vendor records?
  3. How are new employees, role changes, and departures handled?
  4. What information does the firm need to provide when a support issue is escalated?
  5. How are backups, restore tests, and recovery responsibilities documented?
  6. What will the provider coordinate with vendors, and what remains with the firm?

Choose a practical next step

If the firm needs recurring support across users, systems, security, backups, and vendors, an ongoing managed IT conversation may be appropriate. If the immediate need is limited to one office, network, account, or transition project, start by defining that project and its owner.
