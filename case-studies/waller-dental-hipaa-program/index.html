HIPAA program case study

From a document binder to a program people could use

Odyssey connected workforce training, written expectations, risk analysis, remediation ownership, and audit evidence.

Updated August 13, 2026Client-reported outcomeDental practice

The practice already had HIPAA documents. The problem was that those files did not function as a living operating program. Staff activity, policies, risk decisions, and proof of completed work needed to connect.

Starting pointScattered PDF documents
Primary needA usable, documented program
Reported timingCore work in under 30 days

Why the existing documents were not enough

A policy file can show that language exists. It does not show whether employees were trained, whether risks were reviewed, whether corrective work had an owner, or whether evidence could be produced when needed.

Documents were disconnected

Written material existed, but it was not organized around an active review and maintenance process.

Training needed evidence

Completion records needed to be connected to workforce responsibilities and retained for review.

Risk work needed structure

Findings had to move from observation to a documented decision, owner, and next action.

Leadership needed visibility

The practice needed a clearer way to understand what was complete, open, and waiting on evidence.

How Odyssey organized the program

  1. Organize the current stateReview the available policies, training material, records, and known security concerns before prescribing new work.
  2. Connect policies to real workflowsAlign written expectations with the way staff members access systems, communicate, store information, and respond to incidents.
  3. Complete the risk-analysis processDocument relevant risks and the existing safeguards that reduce or manage them.
  4. Assign remediation ownershipTurn findings into tracked actions with a responsible owner, supporting evidence, and a decision trail.
  5. Prepare the maintenance rhythmMake the program easier to revisit as employees, vendors, devices, or workflows change.

What Waller received

Organized workforce training records
Policies aligned to practice workflows
Documented security risk analysis
Remediation and evidence structure
Clearer control-review visibility
A foundation for ongoing updates
Client-reported outcome Training, written policies, and a full risk assessment were implemented in under 30 days.

The practice manager also reported that controls were documented for the next audit.

Why organized evidence mattered

The most important improvement was not producing more files. It was making responsibilities and evidence easier to find. A working program helps leadership see whether an activity was completed, who owns the next step, and what supports the organization’s decision.

Important context: This is one client’s reported experience. Timing and outcomes depend on scope, access, existing documentation, leadership involvement, and the organization’s environment. Odyssey provides compliance program support, not legal advice or a guarantee of regulatory compliance.

Five questions to test your program

Start by asking whether the program can answer five questions: Who was trained? Which policy applies? What risks were identified? Who owns the corrective action? Where is the evidence? If those answers live in separate places or depend on one person’s memory, the program needs stronger operational structure.
